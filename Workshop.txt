description=[h2]Description[/h2]
description=[i]The world ended, sure. Civilization collapsed, absolutely. But does your safehouse have to look like a grey concrete bunker built by someone who's given up on life? Not anymore.
description=Long before Dulux and Sherwin-Williams, humans were already grinding up berries, rust and burnt wood to make their caves less depressing. If the Romans could whitewash an aqueduct, you can whitewash your barricaded gas station.[/i]
description=
description=This mod lets you produce your own paint from scratch using nothing but Quicklime, water, and whatever pigment-adjacent nonsense you can scavenge, forage or scrape off an old piece of metal. No paint store required — just proper (ish) chemistry and a bucket.
description=
description=Every color is built on the same lime-based binder used since Antiquity for limewash — the exact same stuff that's kept Mediterranean houses white for a couple thousand years. And it doesn't stop at the binder: every single pigment recipe in this mod is based on a real historical process. Red and Turquoise are quite literally iron oxide and verdigris, made the same way blacksmiths and painters have made them for centuries — rust an old scrap of metal in vinegar, file off the good stuff, mix it in. Black is carbon pigment from charcoal, the same stuff used in cave paintings 40,000 years ago. There's no hand-waving "grind rock, get paint" nonsense here — if you know your chemistry, you'll recognize every single recipe.
description=
description=[h2]Crafting Process[/h2]
description=
description=[b]Step 1 — Slake your Lime[/b]
description=Before you can paint anything, you need your binder. You'll find this recipe under the vanilla [b]Misc[/b] category — it's just prep work, not painting yet.
description=Requires:[list]
description=[*] 1 Quicklime
description=[*] 0.6L Water
description=[*] 1 Bucket (kept)
description=[/list]
description=[i]Fair warning: quicklime does not care about your safety. The hazard system for this isn't live yet (see "Planned Features" below), but seriously — start gearing up now anyway. Gloves, a dust mask, goggles, every single time you slake lime, no exceptions. Build the habit before the update forces it on you, not after. Nobody wants to lose their eyesight for a week because they got comfortable cutting corners on something that used to be harmless.[/i]
description=
description=[b]Step 2 — Mix your Paint[/b]
description=Once you've got Slaked Lime, you can mix up any of the following base colors directly in an empty paint bucket. All of these live in their own dedicated [b]Paint[/b] crafting category, so you're not digging through unrelated recipes to find your color of choice:
description=
description=[list]
description=[*] [b]White[/b] — 3 Slaked Lime + 1.5L Water
description=[*] [b]Black[/b] — 2 Slaked Lime + 1.5L Water + 1 Charcoal [i](mortar & pestle required)[/i]
description=[*] [b]Yellow[/b] — 2 Slaked Lime + 1.5L Water + 4 Mustard
description=[*] [b]Blue[/b] — 2 Slaked Lime + 1.5L Water + 8 Blueberries [i](mortar & pestle required)[/i]
description=[*] [b]Purple[/b] — 2 Slaked Lime + 1.5L Water + 4 Blueberries + 4 Black Berries [i](mortar & pestle required)[/i]
description=[*] [b]Green[/b] — 2 Slaked Lime + 1.5L Water + 8 Spinach [i](mortar & pestle required)[/i]
description=[*] [b]Pink[/b] — 2 Slaked Lime + 1.5L Water + 8 Strawberries [i](mortar & pestle required)[/i]
description=[*] [b]Brown[/b] — 2 Slaked Lime + 1.5L Water + 3 Clay
description=[*] [b]Red[/b] — 2 Slaked Lime + 1.3L Water + Vinegar + 1 Scrap Metal [i](file required — yes, you're literally making iron oxide, the same pigment humans used 40,000 years ago in cave paintings)[/i]
description=[*] [b]Turquoise[/b] — 2 Slaked Lime + 1.3L Water + Vinegar + 2 Copper Scrap [i](file required — congratulations, you've just made verdigris, the same way the Romans did)[/i]
description=[/list]
description=[b]Step 3 — Blend for Secondary Colors[/b]
description=No pigment for orange growing on trees around here, so you'll just have to remember 4th grade color theory instead:
description=[list]
description=[*] [b]Grey[/b] — White + Black (1:1)
description=[*] [b]Orange[/b] — Red + Yellow (1:1)
description=[*] [b]Cyan[/b] — Blue + Green (1:1)
description=[*] [b]Light Brown[/b] — Brown + White (3:7)
description=[*] [b]Light Blue[/b] — Blue + White (3:7)
description=[/list]
description=Each of these can be mixed straight into an empty bucket, or in bulk by combining two full buckets of paint directly — handy if you'd rather not carry an extra empty bucket around just to babysit a 50/50 mix.
description=
description=[h2]New Item[/h2]
description=
description=[b]Slaked Lime[/b]
description=[i]"Well, you certainly can't paint with rocks. But you can with THIS rock, technically."[/i]
description=
description=The result of dumping water on Quicklime — which, chemically, is exactly as violent as it sounds. Slaked Lime is your binder for every single paint recipe in this mod, standing in for modern chemical binders the same way it's stood in for them for the last few thousand years. It's also, historically, how buildings got whitewashed long before anyone invented the paint roller.
description=
description=Handle it with basic care. It's not radioactive, it's not going to turn you into a zombie, but it will absolutely let you know if you weren't wearing the right gear. Speaking of which:
description=
description=[h2]Planned Features[/h2]
description=[list]
description=[*] [b]Quicklime handling hazards[/b] — Slaking lime without gloves, a dust mask, and goggles is supposed to come with consequences (hand burns, coughing fits, temporary blindness — cumulative, because negligence should hurt a little, just like it does in real life). The Lua script for this isn't implemented yet, but it's coming.
description=[*] Possible milk-based White Paint variant (because "milk paint" — casein paint made from milk and lime — was a real historical technique, not something I made up to sound smart)
